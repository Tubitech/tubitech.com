Ramot Build Blog

Hey Everyone

We are pleased to announce that this year we will start our first build blog. In this build blog, we will tell about our ups and downs throughout the process and share experiences. The blog's target is mostly documenting our process in order to collaborate and share experiences with other teams and younger generations. We will make a thread and answer questions as a group.

About The Team TUBITECH

  • Our team has been competing since 2024 Crescendo. The team was founded as an all-rookie team of 23 people and 2 mentors. Throughout the year, we made immense progress and now, with ~20 new rookies and 8 new mentors, we have 34 team members and 9 mentors.
  • This year, we will compete in 2 different regionals with a strong desire to go to the championship as we did last year.

Reefscape Goals

This year, we want to recruit and train the students around us to create a renewable team. Also, thanks to the mentorship of our new mentors, we want to spend a more professional season and create exceptions by building an extraordinary robot. The main goal of this year is to qualify for the championship by building a new and better robot while adhering to the principle of gracious professionalism.

Off Season

After the FIRST Championship, our team made a meeting and brainstorm about how to make the team more sustainable throughout multiple years. After that, we decided to accelerate "Meet the Deer" conferences and enrichened the content with more technical stuff. We used our Crescendo robot "Velox" to inspire them. Also, our team members had a very rich 9 month period of engineering. With Teknofest projects, such as UAV and UUV, and internship programs made in collaboration with TÜBİTAK (Scientific and Technological Research Council of Turkey) helped a lot throughout the learning progress. Lastly, experienced members of the team made interviews and 1 to 1 educations to whose eager to participate in the team in 2025.

After new members, we contacted with engineers and uni-graduates telling them to mentor our team. Through the search phase, we contacted so many people, learned so many things and 8 new people agreed to mentor us this year. One of them, Ahmet Yaylı, said we can use his workshop's opportunities since we don't have an adequate working place in school. And also, he has an appropriate spirit for FIRST.

WEEK 1 - Strategy

The day after the kickoff, tasks were distributed, the rulebook was read, and important points were noted:

  • When ALGEAs are thrown into the PROCESSOR, the human player of the opposing alliance can pick it up.
  • CORALs cannot be thrown.
  • Game objects in the NET and CORAL are counted at the end of the game. They can be removed by the opposing alliance.

After that, we started to outline the strategy for AUTO and TELEOP.

AUTO

  • Must have: Taxi and 1 L4 CORAL
  • Very nice to have: Taxi and 3 L4 CORAL

To achieve that, we need to have a CORAL system to do L4s and explore how to place that CORAL.

TELEOP

  • Must have:
    • Scoring CORAL at every level of the REEF
    • Intaking CORAL from the SOURCE
    • Intaking ALGEA and scoring it into the PROCESSOR
    • A fast CORAL cycle from SOURCE to the REEF
    • A climb system for any of the CAGE
  • Nice to have:
    • Putting ALGEA to the NET
    • A climb system for DEEP CAGE
    • A chasis strong enough to overcome the impact of the CAGE in case of collision
  • Dream:
    • Being able to pull alliance mates for climbing
    • Double climb system for CAGE
    • Very fast CORAL cycle
    • Launching ALGEA into the NET

WEEK 2

So, we came up with bunch of different scratch models. These models had their own unique point of view, however, there was a model that stood aside. This model had:

  • A 3-stage elevator system
  • A 3-pivoted arm mounted to the elevator
  • A unique intake system for both CORAL and ALGEA
  • More than enough room for climb system

Designers of the team gathered and started to CAD the sub-structures.

Sub-Structure 1: Drive Base

Last year, we had Mk4i swerve modules mounted with Neo v1.1 motors. However, this year we decided to use KrakenX60 motors. This led us to the point we should decide whether to buy Mk4i to Mk4n KrakenX60 conversion kit for 130$ with a pinion gear kit for 28$ *OR* to directly buy Mk4n or Mk4c for 377$ maximum. It seems like the first choice is the economical one, but in order to have spare swerve modules and making enough room for the cage to go inside of the chasis (for CAD purposes), we decided to directly buy Mk4n modules.

With Mk4n and Mk4i modules, we designed 2 different swerve based chasises. They both used aluminum 6063 25mm to 50mm rectangle profiles for outer structure and polycarbonate plate for inner structure.

Sub-Structure 2: Elevator

We decided to use *GreyT Elevator* and bought 1 First Stage and 2 Extra Stages. It operates with a weight reducing single chain lift system lifting the starting stage. The system is flexible enough to change the width or to hardlock the maximum height.

Sub-Structure 3: The Arm

During CAD, we found out that making a 3 pivoted arm is so hard that we cannot do that in our workshop with constrained resources in this limited timeline. So, we decided to use elevator as the first pivot and added one more pivot to the junction of arm and elevator. This way, it's so much easy to CAD, so much more manufacturable and has reduced the complexity of control. With further CAD, we realized that we need just one more pivot to gather CORAL from the ground. So, we added one more pivot to the very tip of the arm.

Sub-Structure 4: Intake

In early stages, we decided to use a fixed intake to get CORALs from the SOURCE, but, during development, our thoughts changed to an arm based intake that also can work as an outlet for faster SOURCE to REEF cycle. This made the structure much more complex, but it was worth it.

In order to achieve this, the design team came up with the idea for what we call *double-intake*. This system has 4 wheels (2 of them each side), and they are rotating at the same speed. First 2 pair is furher away from each other than the other 2, making it shaped like a triangle. This shape allows ALGEA to squish and grabs it hard enough. With that, we can adjust the place we want to put the ALGEA and decide between the NET and PROCESSOR freely. And also, this shape is an effective choice for CORAL intake, because, second pair of wheels has just enough room for a CORAL. So, the piece sticks there the way we wanted and waits for us to shoot it to the CORAL.

While our CAD team were workin on these structures, our other team mates started to make the field parts according to the CAD data that FIRST has shared. We used *fiberboard* and some screws for the field.

At the third and forth weeks, Turkish semester break came up and everybody started to scatter to their house. While semester, our CAD team finalized the design of both robot super-structure and the robocart. During this process, several design iterations were made to maximize the robot's functionality and identify the mechanisms that best fit our strategy. At the end of the semester, we made a finalized *Need List* and seperated the tasks to our team mates accordingly.

WEEK 5

Field team finally made the PROCESSOR and almost finished the REEF, SOURCE and BARGE. Also, we found an old mattress for the field at our school’s dining hall to use as the field’s base.

Let’s talk about our robot. While we wait for the new Mk4n Swerve Modules we ordered, we made an Mk4i according chasis in order to see if any problem occurs during assembly. The chasis were 700mm to 800mm and made by 25mm to 50mm aluminum box profiles. Also, we made a rectangle from the same profiles at the middle of the chasis for elevator assembly. Lastly, a polycarbonate plate were cut accordingly for the base of the robot. This plate is both for mechanical strength and for electronic components.

Another task force of our, what we call “The Machiners” started to take quotes from various companies for the steel and aluminum custom made parts. Also, this team were responsible for gathering all the needs we can get from local machining shops such as bearings and aluminum profiles. Once all the quotes are discussed, we sent the parts to various different companies and started to wait.

WEEK 6

Our field team finally finished REEF, SOURCE and BARGE. Also, we built a drivers’ station. The team found a suitable area for the field and assembled the field parts to there. With that, we had a field like place for further tests.

The machining team gathered all the custom made parts from companies and started to assemble them. Firstly, the shafts came from lathe; secondly, aluminum plates came from the laser machine. Also, they produced required spacers from 3D printers. With all these combined, production of *Gripper* sub-structure had been done with a very good pace. With the mechanical structure of *Gripper* had finished and the electronic team took the responsibility of assembling Neo v1.1 Brushless motors and 4in Stealth Wheels. However, at the further weeks, we discovered that this exceeds the maximum amount the robot can expand and switched to 2in Stealth Wheels for the outer couple.

Mechanical team has started to assemble WCP GreyT Cascade Elevator. We used 1in to 2in aluminum box profiles for this mission and finished assembling except the sprocket and custom gearbox systems.

To gather *Intake*, *Arm* and *Elevator* we bought 4 Kraken X60 motors from local teams. With these motors, we started to finalize some of the sub-structures. With the Krakens installed, custom aluminum parts were connected to 30mm to 30mm sigma profiles with knurled nuts and 2 pivot points were formed. The other pair of Krakens were used for the base of the elevator in the following weeks.

The software team also started to work. They started to write the codes for sub-structures individually. Moreover, they perfected the swerve drive codes from last year and integrated it for this year’s robot
